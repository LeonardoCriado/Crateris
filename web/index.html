Crateris tests whether a single pretrained foundation model can forecast many previously unseen time series without training a separate model for each series.

How does zero-shot forecasting work?

The model was pretrained on billions of time-series points across domains, and forecasts a new series without training on it — like an LLM answering without fine-tuning. The difference with ARIMA (classical statistics: one fitted model per series, linearity assumptions, needs enough history) and Meta's Prophet (additive trend + seasonality + holidays model, also fitted per series with manual configuration) is that here there is a single model for everything, at the cost of lower interpretability and more conservative behavior on regime breaks.

The trade-offs

  • Fewer individual models to maintain
  • Less manual configuration
  • Multiple frequencies out of the box
  • Lower interpretability
  • Weaker reaction to regime breaks
  • Higher compute cost per forecast

Backtest
Train on all but the last N points, forecast, compare against observed values (N = published horizon).
Horizon
12 steps monthly, 28 business days daily, 24 hours hourly.
Context
Truncated to the most recent 512 points per series.
MAE
Mean absolute error, in series units. Lower is better.
RMSE
Root mean squared error; penalizes large errors more than MAE.
MAPE
Mean absolute percentage error. Unreliable near zero; shown as “—” when undefined.
Inference
Local CPU, no GPUs, no per-series training.
